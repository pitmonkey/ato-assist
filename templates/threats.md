# Threat picture

Who would act against this system, what they can do, and what they would go after. The assessor writes this. Nothing in the plugin fills it in, because a threat picture asserted by a tool is one nobody agreed to.

`/ato-risk` asks "Who or what would act?" for every risk it drafts. This file is where that answer comes from; without it the threat half of each risk is invented at the point of writing.

`ato status` counts the `threat-mapping` phase unmet while the line below is still here. Delete it once the picture is real.

## Who would act

The threat actors or sources that matter for this system, in the assessor's words — an insider with privileged access, a criminal group after the data, an untargeted scanner. Only the ones the assessment can defend.

## What they can do

The capability each one realistically has. This is what keeps a rating honest: a threat nobody can execute is not the same risk as one that needs a script and a weekend.

## What they would go after

The data, function or interface each actor would target, and why losing it matters to the business. This is where a risk's consequence comes from.

## What this rests on

Where the picture came from — the interview, an ingested source, the assessor's own judgement. A threat with no basis is a hunch, and saying so is better than dressing it up.
